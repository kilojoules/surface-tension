The constraint to avoid explicit `for` and `while` loops forces a shift from imperative programming to a functional style. While explicit loops are often more readable for complex state changes, using comprehensions and built-in higher-order functions can lead to concise, expression-oriented code that leverages Python's internal optimizations. For this problem, I will use `itertools.product` to generate all possible combinations of starting positions and intervals, and list comprehensions to calculate the counts of matching building heights.
