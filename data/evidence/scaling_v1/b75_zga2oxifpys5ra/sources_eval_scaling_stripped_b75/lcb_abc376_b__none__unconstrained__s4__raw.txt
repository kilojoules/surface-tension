On a ring of size $N$, if one hand is at $O$, the other hand is constrained to a linear path of $N-1$ nodes. The distance between $S$ and $E$ on this linear path is simply the absolute difference of their positions when the ring is "cut" at $O$.

If we map the positions such that $O$ is at $0$ (or $N$), the positions become $(S-O) \pmod N$ and $(E-O) \pmod N$. The distance is then $|(S-O) \pmod N - (E-O) \pmod N|$.

Example: $N=6, L=1, R=4$. Move $R$ to $6$.
$O=L=1$.
$S = (4-1) \pmod 6 = 3$.
$E = (6-1) \pmod 6 = 5$.
Dist $= |3-5| = 2$. (Path: $4 \to 3 \to 2 \to 1$ is blocked, so $4 \to 5 \to 6$).
