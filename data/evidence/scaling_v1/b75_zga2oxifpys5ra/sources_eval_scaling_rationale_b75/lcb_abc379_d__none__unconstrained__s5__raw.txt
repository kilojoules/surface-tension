The constraint to avoid explicit loops forces a shift toward a functional programming paradigm, emphasizing declarative data transformations over imperative state changes. While not always more "idiomatic" for every task, it encourages the use of higher-order functions and prevents side-effect-driven bugs. To solve this problem without loops, I will use `functools.reduce` to maintain the state (the set of plants and the current time offset) across the sequence of queries, and `bisect_left` to efficiently count and remove plants that have reached the required height.

            bisect.insort(plants, -t_offset)

            return self
