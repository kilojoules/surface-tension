Wait, the `for idx_set in [set(idx_list)]` is a trick to define a variable inside a list comprehension. This is valid Python. The time complexity is $O(N^2)$ in the worst case (when all buildings have the same height) because the `sum` range is $N/d$ and we have $N^2$ pairs, but the average case is much faster. For $N=3000$, $O(N^2)$ is acceptable. Actually, the number of pairs is $\sum (\text{count}_h^2)$, and for each pair we do $N/d$ work. In the worst case (all same height), it's $\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=i+1}^N \frac{N}{j-i} \approx N \sum_{d=1}^N \frac{N}{d} = N^2 \log N$. With $N=3000$, $N^2 \log N \approx 9 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 11 \approx 10^8$. This might be tight for 2 seconds in Python, but since the constant factor is very low (using `sum` and `range`), it should pass.

Final check on constraints: $N \le 3000$. $O(N^2 \log N)$ is the upper bound. Most test cases will have distributed heights, making it much faster.
