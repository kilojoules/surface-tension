The constraint to avoid explicit loops and recursion forces a shift from imperative state-mutation to a functional paradigm, emphasizing the transformation of data streams. In this specific problem, the goal is to find the minimum number of operations to sort a permutation. An operation at index $k$ sorts everything to the left and everything to the right of $k$. This means if we can find a $k$ such that all elements $\{1, \dots, k-1\}$ are to the left of $k$ and all elements $\{k+1, \dots, N\}$ are to the right of $k$, one operation suffices. Otherwise, if the permutation is not already sorted, it can be proven that at most 2 operations are always sufficient for $N \ge 3$.

To implement this without loops, I will use `sys.stdin.read().split()` to gather all input, `map` for transformations, and list comprehensions (which are expressions, not statements) to process the test cases. Specifically, I will check if the permutation is already sorted (0 operations), if there exists a $k$ that splits the permutation into two sets of values (1 operation), and otherwise return 2.
