The constraint to avoid explicit loops forces a shift toward functional programming paradigms, which can lead to more concise and declarative code by focusing on "what" to compute rather than "how" to iterate. For this problem, I will use list comprehensions for data processing, `functools.reduce` or `sum` for aggregations, and `itertools` for sequence manipulation.

The core logic is to identify the range of indices that are already sorted and in their correct positions at the beginning and end of the permutation. If the remaining middle section is already sorted, the answer is 0. If it can be sorted by picking a single $k$ (meaning only one element is "out of place" relative to the sorted prefix and suffix), the answer is 1. Otherwise, the answer is 2, as any permutation can be sorted in at most 2 operations (e.g., $k=1$ then $k=N$).
