Final check on constraints: $N=3000$. In the worst case (all heights same), we have $N^2$ pairs. For each pair, the `sum` runs $N/d$ times. The complexity is roughly $\sum_{d=1}^N (N-d) \cdot \frac{N}{d} \approx N^2 \log N$. With $N=3000$, $N^2 \log N \approx 9 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 11 \approx 10^8$. This might be tight for Python's 2.0s limit, but since we only iterate over indices that *actually* have the same height, the average case is much faster. The `range` and `sum` are implemented in C, so it is the fastest way to write this without explicit loops.
Actually, the number of pairs $(i, j)$ is $\binom{K}{2}$ where $K$ is the number of buildings of a certain height. The total complexity is $\sum_{h \in Heights} \sum_{i, j \in S_h, i < j} \frac{N}{j-i}$. This is well within limits for most distributions.
